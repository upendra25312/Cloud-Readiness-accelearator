## Slide 2 — The Challenge You Are Facing

**Heading:** Moving to cloud is not the hard part — deciding *which* cloud, *when*, and *at what cost* is

**Three columns (icons + short text):**

**Column 1 — Too Many Opinions:**  
Every hyperscaler says they are the best choice for your estate. Microsoft, AWS, and Google all have free tools and pre-sales teams telling you different things. Without independent analysis, the loudest voice wins.

**Column 2 — Complexity You Cannot See:**  
You likely have Oracle databases, end-of-life operating systems, licensing obligations, and infrastructure dependencies that will affect cost and timelines — but are only visible once you look. Most cloud cost estimates are wrong because they start before the data is collected.

**Column 3 — The Cost of Getting It Wrong:**  
Organisations that start cloud migrations without a formal assessment overspend by 30–60% in Year 1. The recovery cost — rewinding a migration, renegotiating a hyperscaler commitment, or handling a licensing surprise — is typically 3–5× the cost of a proper upfront assessment.

**Bottom callout (Rackspace Red):**  
> "A cloud migration decision is a board-level financial commitment of £1M–£10M+. It deserves the same rigour as any capital investment."

**Speaker notes:**  
The customer's IT Director or CTO may already know this — validate it. The goal of this slide is to establish that the CRA exists because these problems are real and predictable. Every customer who has skipped a structured assessment has encountered one or more of these issues. The question is whether they encounter it before or after they have committed to a hyperscaler.

## Slide 3 — What Is a Cloud Readiness Assessment

**Heading:** A Cloud Readiness Assessment (CRA) is not a report — it is a decision-making system

**Left panel (what it is):**
- A four-phase engagement (Discovery → Analysis → Evaluation → Planning)
- Typically 16–20 weeks for a mid-market estate (200–500 applications)
- Uses proven tooling: Azure Migrate, Google Migration Center, AWS Application Discovery Service
- Produces 8+ formal deliverables — not a slide deck, but board-grade documentation
- Fully cloud-neutral: the framework recommends whichever cloud the data supports

**Right panel (what it produces):**

| Deliverable | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Infrastructure & Application Inventory | Definitive record of your estate — scope, complexity, dependencies |
| Cloud Readiness Scores | Per-application traffic-light readiness rating |
| Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Analysis | 7-layer financial model covering all cost scenarios |
| Hyperscaler Evaluation | Scored comparison: AWS vs Azure vs GCP for your specific estate |
| Hyperscaler Recommendation | Evidence-based recommendation with financial justification |
| Risk Register | Technical, licensing, and delivery risks identified and scored |
| Migration Wave Plan | Sequenced application migration roadmap |
| Part 2 Entry Point | The commercial document that initiates the migration engagement |

**Speaker notes:**  
Emphasise the word "system." A report is something someone reads once and puts in a drawer. What you are describing is a structured process that produces a series of working artefacts that the customer's IT team will use during the migration. The application inventory becomes the source of truth. The TCO model becomes the CFO reference. The wave plan becomes the programme plan. These are tools, not documents.

## Slide 7 — Phase 2: Analysis — Making Sense of the Data

**Heading:** Phase 2 Analysis — Turning data into a readiness picture

**Left panel:**

**Cloud Readiness Scoring — 5 dimensions per application:**

| Dimension | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| Technical Complexity | Containerisation readiness, OS compatibility, architecture patterns |
| Business Criticality | RTO/RPO requirements, tier classification, business impact |
| Data Sensitivity | Regulatory, compliance, sovereignty constraints (GDPR, FCA, etc.) |
| Dependency Risk | Tightly coupled dependencies that constrain cloud placement |
| Licence Risk | Oracle, Microsoft, IBM licences with cloud mobility restrictions |

**Output: Four readiness categories per application:**
- **Cloud Ready** — migrate with minimal change (rehost/lift-and-shift)
- **Cloud Friendly** — minor modifications needed (replatform)
- **Cloud Challenged** — significant work required (rearchitect)
- **Blocked** — retain on-premises or specialist path required

**Right panel — Governance Maturity Assessment:**

Rackspace conducts a 2–3 hour governance workshop with your IT Director to assess cloud governance readiness across 6 domains:
- Identity and Access Management (IAM)
- Security and Compliance
- Change and Release Management
- Monitoring and Observability
- Cost Management and FinOps
- Operations Readiness

**Output:** Governance Maturity Score (1–5 scale) + Gap Register + recommendations for cloud-ready governance state

**Speaker notes:**  
The readiness scoring is the output that surprises customers most. They tend to think most of their applications are "cloud ready." In practice, 15–25% are typically "cloud challenged" and 5–10% are "blocked" in mid-market estates. The value is in knowing this before you commit to a migration programme — not after the first wave is already in flight.

## Slide 8 — Phase 3: Evaluation — The Financial Model

**Heading:** Phase 3 Evaluation — A 7-layer cost model you can present to your board

**Heading callout (Rackspace Red):** Most cloud cost estimates only cover 2 of these 7 layers.

**7-layer table:**

| Layer | What It Models | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Like-for-Like Baseline** | Exact current specification, lifted to cloud | The starting point — shows the cost of doing nothing extra |
| **(b) Optimised / Right-Sized** | Cloud-right-sized instances based on P95 utilisation data | Typically 20–35% lower than like-for-like |
| **(c) Delta (b minus a)** | The financial benefit of right-sizing alone | Headline "cloud saving" number |
| **(d) Licensing Overlay** | SQL Server Azure Hybrid Benefit, Oracle BYOL, Windows AHB | Often the single largest cost item in Microsoft-heavy estates |
| **(e) On-Premises Status Quo** | Current hardware + maintenance + facilities + software over 3 years | Establishes the true cost of staying on-premises |
| **(f) Year 1 Dual-Running** | Cloud costs + remaining on-prem costs during migration overlap | Prevents the "it cost more in Year 1 than expected" problem |
| **(g) Partner Discounts & Credits** | AMM / MAP / PSO funding applied | Net cost after partner funding — the board-level headline number |

**Bottom line:**  
> **The difference between layers (a) and (g) is typically 40–60% for a Microsoft-heavy estate with AMM funding applied.**

**Speaker notes:**  
Walk through each layer. The licensing overlay (d) is where the largest surprises are — customers often underestimate the Azure Hybrid Benefit saving on SQL Server licences. On a 500-server estate with significant SQL Server, this can be a £500K+ three-year saving. Layer (e) is what makes the "stay on-prem" option visible — customers rarely have a full view of their current infrastructure costs including hardware refresh, data centre costs, and maintenance. Layer (g) is the number that goes to the board.

## Slide 9 — Phase 3: Evaluation — The Hyperscaler Decision

**Heading:** Phase 3 Evaluation — How we choose the right cloud for your estate

**Key principle (prominent callout):**  
> "The recommendation follows the evidence. We do not start with a preferred cloud and build a case for it. We build the evidence and let it lead to the recommendation."

**Evaluation framework:**

| Evaluation Criterion | Weight | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Technical compatibility with your estate | Agreed with you before scoring | Most critical — applications that cannot run on a given cloud are automatic disqualifiers |
| 3-year Total Cost of Ownership | Agreed with you before scoring | The financial case for and against each option |
| Regulatory and compliance fit | Agreed with you before scoring | Data sovereignty, FCA, GDPR, sector-specific requirements |
| Azure Hybrid Benefit / licensing optimisation | Varies by estate | Microsoft-heavy estates often have a structural Azure cost advantage |
| Migration complexity and tooling | Agreed with you before scoring | How hard each migration path actually is, not just the cloud pricing |
| Hyperscaler roadmap and services | Agreed with you before scoring | Which cloud's services roadmap best fits your strategic direction |

**Key message:**  
- Criteria weights are agreed with you before any scoring begins — this prevents bias and protects the credibility of the recommendation
- All three hyperscalers are evaluated and scored — we do not eliminate options before the evidence is presented
- The recommendation slide in your executive report comes after the evidence slides — not before

**Speaker notes:**  
The "criteria agreed before scoring" point is critical. In several engagements, customers have asked "how do I know you haven't already decided which cloud to recommend?" The answer is that the weighting methodology is agreed in writing before any scoring is done. If the customer wants to emphasise regulatory compliance over cost, we adjust the weighting. The scoring is transparent and auditable.

## Slide 10 — Phase 4: Planning — From Recommendation to Migration Roadmap

**Heading:** Phase 4 Planning — The bridge from assessment to migration

**Three outputs:**

**Output 1 — Migration Wave Plan**  
Applications sequenced into migration waves based on:
- Cloud readiness score (Cloud Ready apps go first)
- Business criticality (Tier-3 applications tested first; Tier-1 last)
- Dependency clusters (applications that must move together)
- Target environment readiness (landing zone prerequisites)

Wave 0: Proof of Concept (5–10% of estate, non-production, 8–12 weeks)  
Wave 1: Low-complexity cloud-ready applications (Tier 2/3)  
Wave 2–3: Medium-complexity applications  
Wave 4: Business-critical Tier-1 applications  
Wave 5+: Retained / Oracle / specialist workloads

**Output 2 — Risk Register**  
11 categories of risk documented, scored, and mitigated:
Technical, Dependency, Licensing (Oracle), Data Quality, End-of-Life OS, Regulatory, Schedule, Resource, Commercial, Stakeholder, Third-Party

**Output 3 — Part 2 Entry Point**  
The commercial document that defines the migration engagement:
- Scope and wave sequence
- Timeline and resource model
- Investment summary (net of partner funding)
- Hyperscaler partner registration status
- Next steps and signatures

**Speaker notes:**  
Phase 4 is where the assessment becomes a programme. The wave plan is the output that the customer's IT Programme Manager will use to manage the migration. Emphasise that the sequencing is deliberate — we do not put your most critical applications in Wave 0. The Proof of Concept wave is specifically designed to validate the migration tooling and process before any business-critical workloads are touched.

## Appendix A — Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What size estate does CRA work for?**  
CRA is designed for organisations with 50–2,000 applications and 200–5,000 VMs. Below 50 applications, a SMART assessment or lighter-touch evaluation may be more proportionate. Above 2,000 applications, the timeline and team size scale accordingly.

**Q: Can we run CRA if we already know which cloud we want?**  
Yes. CRA produces the documentation required to justify that decision to your board and to qualify for partner funding programmes — even if you are confident in the outcome. The evidence-based approach protects you if the recommendation is challenged.

**Q: What if we have Oracle workloads?**  
Oracle workloads (particularly Oracle RAC, Oracle E-Business Suite, and Oracle Database Enterprise Edition) require specialist licensing analysis. Rackspace engages its Oracle practice team at Phase 1 when Oracle is identified in the inventory. This does not extend the timeline for non-Oracle workloads.

**Q: What is the total cost of a CRA engagement?**  
CRA is a fixed-fee engagement. The exact fee depends on estate size, complexity (Oracle, SAP, regulatory requirements), and timeline. Net of partner programme funding (AMM/MAP/PSO), the investment is typically recovered within the first year of migration savings. Your Rackspace Pre-Sales Architect will provide a fixed-fee proposal following the scoping workshop.

**Q: What happens after the CRA — what is "Part 2"?**  
"Part 2" is the migration engagement that follows the assessment. The Part 2 Entry Point document (delivered at the end of Phase 4) defines the scope, timeline, and investment for executing the migration. Rackspace can deliver Part 2 directly, co-deliver with your hyperscaler, or hand off to another delivery partner of your choice.
